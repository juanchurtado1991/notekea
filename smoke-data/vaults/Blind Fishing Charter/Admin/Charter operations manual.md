# Charter operations manual

How I run a trip, written down so Luis can run it the same way when I am not aboard, and so I stop
reinventing the rules every season.

## Before the trip
- Confirm the booking the evening before by text or email. Check the deposit status on the month's booking
  note ([[November 2026]], [[December 2026]]).
- Check the marine forecast at 17:00 and again at 5:00. My limit for half days on the reef is 20 knots; for
  offshore days it is 18 knots or seas over 4 ft. Above that I cancel and log it in [[Weather cancellations]].
- Ice: 6 bags for a half day, 10 bags for a full day. Bait ordered two days ahead.
- Fuel the boat the day before, never the morning of. Log every fill-up in the fuel log of that boat.

## At the dock
- Safety briefing before we leave the slip: life jackets, life raft, fire extinguishers, VHF, where the first
  aid kit is, and who to call if I am hurt. Show at least one client how to use the radio.
- Check the licenses in the wallet: the client fishing license is covered by the boat's vessel license, so
  clients do not need their own.
- Max 6 passengers, no exceptions, even for kids.

## On the water
- Reef: fish the outer reef first, move inside if it is rough. Respect the closed areas and the spawning
  closures; no keeping amberjack while the season is closed.
- Offshore: troll the weed lines, then look for birds. Run back early if the wind builds.
- Skiff: two anglers max, no running in the shallows on a falling tide, pole the last stretch.
- Photos of every fish for the client, and a quick note for the catch log as soon as we are back.

## Money
- Deposits and refunds follow the [[Deposit policy]].
- The balance is paid at the dock. Card fee is on me, not on the client.
- Tips are cash, split on the dock the same day between the crew who worked, never pooled for later.
- Crew is paid by the end of the month, see the crew pay notes.
- Kids under 10 fish free on half day reef trips, two per trip at most.

## Fuel surcharge
This is the part I forget every time diesel moves. When the last diesel fill-up in the Salt Ledger fuel log
was above $5.00 a gallon, add a $75 fuel surcharge to every full day offshore trip. Half days and skiff trips
never carry a surcharge. Tell the client when they book if possible, and always before the trip, never at
the dock as a surprise.

## After the trip
- Wash down, flush the engines, check the oil, top off the fresh water.
- Write the catch log the same evening, while I remember the numbers.
- Collect and enter the balance, note any unpaid invoice on the client note (balance: unpaid).

## Emergencies
- Man overboard: shout, point, throw the ring, mark the GPS, turn back. Practice it with new crew.
- Injury: first aid kit, call on channel 16, head to the nearest marina with an ambulance access.
- Engine failure: anchor if safe, call the towing service, then report to Dwayne if there is any damage.
