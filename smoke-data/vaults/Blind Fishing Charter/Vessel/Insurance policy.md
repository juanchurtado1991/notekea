# Insurance policy (Salt Ledger)

My own summary of the policy, written from the PDF so I do not have to dig through 30 pages every time.
Agent: Dwayne Pruitt at Harbor Line Insurance Brokers. Policy CAM-H-40816, Coral Anchor Marine Mutual.

## 1. What is covered
The policy covers the vessel Salt Ledger only: hull, machinery, permanently attached equipment and the
tender if I ever get one. The Little Ledger is not covered under this policy; it has its own policy with a
different company, and that paperwork is in the truck, not filed here.

Covered uses: commercial charter fishing for up to 6 paying passengers, plus personal use. Not covered: racing,
carrying cargo for hire, renting the boat out bareboat, or using it as a live-aboard.

## 2. Agreed hull value
The agreed hull value is $185,000. In a total loss they pay the agreed value, no depreciation argument. The
value is reviewed at each renewal and may need a fresh survey (see section 9).

## 3. Liability (P&I)
Protection and indemnity limit: $1,000,000 per occurrence. This covers injury to passengers and crew and
damage I cause to other boats, docks and property. Passenger liability is part of this limit, not on top of
it. Crew injury on the job falls under the Jones Act endorsement that comes with the P&I section.

Medical payments: up to $25,000 per person, regardless of fault, for anyone hurt aboard.

## 4. Towing and assistance
Towing and on-water assistance up to $2,500 per event. I still keep the separate towing membership because
it is faster on the phone, but the policy will reimburse if I use someone else.

## 5. Uninsured boater
Covers my passengers if an uninsured boater hits us, up to $250,000. Rare, but the Keys on a holiday
weekend are what they are.

## 6. Equipment and personal effects
Fishing gear, electronics and tackle are covered up to $15,000, subject to the standard deductible.
Clients' personal effects (phones, sunglasses, their own rods) are covered up to $500 per person, only when
the loss happens because of a covered accident. Something just dropped overboard is not a covered accident.

## 7. Wear, tear and exclusions
Not covered: wear and tear, gradual deterioration, marine growth, osmosis, electrolysis, mechanical breakdown
that is not caused by an accident, freezing, and damage from lack of reasonable maintenance. Keep the
maintenance records (see [[Vessel/Maintenance]]) because the adjuster will ask for them on any engine claim.

## 8. Claims
Report a claim within 24 hours to the claims line, 1-800-555-0163, and to Dwayne. Take photos before
moving anything if it is safe to do so. Do not admit fault to anyone. Keep receipts for emergency repairs:
they reimburse reasonable costs to prevent further damage.

## 9. Survey requirement
A condition and value survey is required every three years and before any renewal where the hull value
changes. Last survey was in 2024. The next one is the haul-out on 2026-12-02 at Matecumbe Boatyard. If the
survey lists recommendations, they must be fixed within 60 days or the insurer can restrict coverage.

## 10. Hurricane plan
I must keep a written hurricane plan on file with them. When a hurricane watch is issued for the Upper Keys,
the boat must be hauled out or moved to the approved hurricane hole (the canal behind the boatyard). If I
haul out because of a named storm, they reimburse 50% of the haul-out cost, up to $1,500 per season.

## 11. Deductibles
Standard deductible: 1% of the agreed hull value, $1,850 per claim. That applies to hull, machinery and
equipment claims. No deductible on liability or medical payments.

Named windstorm deductible: 5% of the agreed hull value, applied to any hull damage from a named storm (a
tropical storm or hurricane given a name by the National Hurricane Center), from the moment a watch or warning
is issued until 72 hours after it ends. The standard deductible does not stack on top of it.

## 12. Navigation limits
Covered waters: Florida coastal waters, within 50 nautical miles of the coast, including the Gulf side and
Florida Bay. The Bahamas are excluded unless I buy a trip endorsement in advance. Trips to the Dry Tortugas
are covered only if I notify the underwriter, Renata Solis, at least 72 hours before departure, in writing
(email is fine), with the dates and the number of passengers.

## 13. Named operators
Only the named operators may run the boat: Tom Marrow and Luis Abreu. Anyone else at the helm, even for a
few minutes with me aboard, can void the hull coverage for that event unless they are added by endorsement
first. Deckhands may work the deck but may not operate the vessel.

## 14. Premium and payment
Annual premium: $6,840, paid in four equal installments due on 03-01, 06-01, 09-01 and 12-01. Paid on time so
far this policy year. Late payment over 15 days suspends coverage until paid.

## 15. Renewal
Renewal date 2027-03-01. Dwayne sends the renewal quote about 45 days before. Things to ask at renewal:
whether adding Kenny as a named operator is worth the extra premium once he gets his license, and whether the
hull value should go up after the new electronics.
