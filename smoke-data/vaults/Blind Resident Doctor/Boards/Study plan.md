# Step 3 study plan

Exam days are in [[Exam registration]]. Week-by-week topics are in [[Boards/Schedule]].
Scores so far are in [[Practice scores]].

## Why this plan
Last year I studied in bursts after bad call nights and it showed on the ITE (see
[[ITE results]]). This year the rule is small and daily: one block of questions every day,
even post-call, even if it is only twenty questions. Long weekend sessions are for review, not
for new material.

## Resources
- Question bank (main resource), all systems, timed and random
- My own flashcard deck, built only from missed questions
- The biostatistics formula sheet I wrote during the ambulatory block
- One review book for ethics and patient safety, read in the train to the hospital

## Daily routine
1. Morning, before pre-rounds if I am on a light rotation: read yesterday's missed questions.
2. Evening: one timed block, then review every explanation, right or wrong.
3. Add every missed concept to the flashcard deck the same night.
4. Ten minutes of flashcards before sleep.

On call days the routine shrinks to flashcards only. Post-call days: a half block at most.

## Rules for reviewing a block
- Write the reason I missed each question: knowledge gap, misread, or changed my answer.
- If I changed a right answer to a wrong one more than twice in a block, stop changing answers.
- Tag the card with the system so weak areas show up in the counts.

## Practice forms
- One full practice form every three weeks, always timed, always on a day off.
- Untimed forms do not count toward the trend. They are for learning only.
- After each form, update [[Weak topics]] and move one study week if a topic falls behind.

## Wellness
- Sleep beats the last block of the night. If it is past 23:00, close the laptop.
- Run on Saturday mornings along the Jamaica Pond loop.
- Dinner with [[Priya Raman]] once a week, no medicine talk for the first hour.

## Study group
[[Priya Raman]] takes Step 3 in February, so we quiz each other on Wednesdays over video.
We split the biostatistics chapter: she makes the cards on study design, I make the cards on
test characteristics.

## Questions to ask the chief
- Can I keep Sunday mornings free on the MICU block before the exam?
- Is there a program fund for the CCS add-on?

## CCS cases
The CCS part is on Day 2. It is a simulation: order tests, move the clock, change location,
counsel the patient. Most people lose points by forgetting the simple orders (vitals, IV
access, monitoring) and the counseling at the end.

- Do the CCS cases on Sundays, two or three at a time.
- Keep a checklist of the orders that every case needs.
- Goal: 36 CCS cases before the exam, at least half of them in the emergency setting.
- Practice the last week with the clock and no notes.
