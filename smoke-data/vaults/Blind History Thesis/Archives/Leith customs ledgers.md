# Leith customs ledgers

Working transcription notes for the Leith collector's quarterly accounts (NRS E504/22). I am only
taking down timber and flax entries from Baltic ports, with ship, master, port of loading, cargo and
the merchant the goods were consigned to. Photographs are in the thesis folder, numbered by visit.

## Method
- One row per ship per quarter. If a ship came in twice in a quarter, two rows.
- Timber is recorded in "loads" (50 cubic feet) in the ledgers; deals and battens are counted separately
  and I convert them only in the spreadsheet, never here.
- Flax is in tons and hundredweights; I keep the ledger's own figures.
- Spelling of masters' names follows the ledger, even when it changes from one quarter to the next.
- Where the clerk's hand is unclear I put the reading in square brackets with a question mark.

## Ledger E504/22/9 (1830–1833)
Thin years for timber. Most Baltic arrivals carry flax and hemp from Riga and Pernau. The merchant
house of Ramsay & Ord appears from the second quarter of 1831, at first with small flax cargoes.
- 1831 Q2: the *Providence* of Leith, master Wm. Gray, from Riga, 42 tons flax, to Ramsay & Ord.
- 1832 Q3: the *Hope*, master J. Kerr, from Pernau, 31 tons flax and 8 tons hemp.
- 1833 Q1: no Baltic arrivals at all (ice).
Overall the flax trade is steady and the timber trade is close to nothing before the duty changes.

## Ledger E504/22/11 (1834–1836)
This is where the timber starts. Memel and Danzig are the main ports of loading.
- 1834 Q2: the *Margaret and Ann*, master T. Brown, from Memel, 1,180 loads of timber, to Ramsay & Ord.
- 1834 Q3: the *Providence* again, from Danzig, 960 loads.
- 1835 Q2: four ships from Memel in six weeks; the clerk notes "much timber lying on the shore".
- 1835 Q4: the *Thetis*, master [Grieve?], from Memel, 1,410 loads.
- 1836 Q1: nothing, ice again.
Pages 44 to 51 of this volume are water-damaged and partly unreadable; I have marked the gaps in the
spreadsheet rather than guessing. Morag thinks the letter book CE57/1/12 may fill some of them.

## Ledger E504/22/14 (1841–1844)
Mostly flax again. The flax comes in larger lots and more of it is consigned to Kirkcaldy and Dundee
spinners through Leith agents, which fits what [[The Flax Frontier]] says about the east-coast mills.
- 1841 Q3: the *Fortitude*, from Riga, 88 tons flax, to a Kirkcaldy spinner via Ramsay & Ord.
- 1842 Q2: timber arrivals fall sharply after the change in duties; only two ships from Memel.
- 1843 Q2: the *Hope* (new master, A. Dow) from Pernau, 57 tons flax.
- 1844 Q4: a cargo of Riga flax "damaged by sea water, allowed abatement of duty".
The fall in timber after 1842 is the core of [[Chapter 3 Timber duties]]; I need the Board of Trade
returns (BT6/231) to set the Leith figures against the national picture.

## Things to check
- Whether "loads" and "lasts" are being used interchangeably by one clerk in 1835 (they should not be).
- Whether Ramsay & Ord are acting as merchants or only as agents for the Kirkcaldy cargoes.
- Masters' names: Gray / Grey, Kerr / Ker, Dow / Dowe.
- The odd entry in 1835 Q2 where the duty paid looks far too low for the cargo; possibly a bonded cargo.

## Running summary
Across the three ledgers I have taken down 214 ship entries so far. Timber peaks in 1835–36 and
collapses after 1842; flax grows steadily through the 1840s. Ramsay & Ord are consigned more cargoes
than any other house in the 1830s.

Largest single timber cargo in the whole run so far: the brig *Janet* of Leith, master R. Hay, from
Memel in 1836 Q3, 2,340 loads, consigned to Ramsay & Ord. Nothing else comes close; the next largest
is the *Thetis* in 1835. Worth a paragraph in Chapter 3 on how far one house was exposed to the trade.
