# Annual review

The School's annual progress review for third-year PhD students. Notes from the handbook, from my
second-year review, and what I need to do this time.

## How it works
Each year every research student submits a short progress report and meets a small panel. The panel
reads the report and one piece of written work, asks questions for about forty minutes, and then
recommends whether the student continues as planned, continues with conditions, or is referred to
the postgraduate director. For third-year students the panel also looks hard at the plan for
completion, because funding ends at the end of the fourth year and the School wants to see a
realistic route to submission.

The report covers: work done since the last review, chapters drafted with word counts, archive work
still to do, training and teaching, conferences and papers, and a timetable to submission. It should
be no more than four pages plus the timetable. The written work is usually a chapter; I will send
the cut version of [[Chapter 2 Leith merchants]].

## Last year (second-year review, 2026-02-20)
The outcome was "continue as planned". The panel liked the archive work and the use of the customs
ledgers, but said three things:
1. The thesis risks being two theses, one on timber and one on flax. I need a clearer argument that
   ties them together through the merchant houses.
2. Chapter 2 was already long in outline. (It is now much longer, so this will come up again.)
3. I should present work outside Edinburgh before the third-year review. Bergen and Utrecht cover
   that, and Lisbon if the funding comes through.
