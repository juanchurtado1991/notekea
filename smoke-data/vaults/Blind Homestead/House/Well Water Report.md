# Well Water Report

Copied from the paper report the lab mailed, with my own notes. Sample ID WR-26-0817, Standard Rural
Panel. Sample taken at the kitchen tap after running it cold for five minutes, carried to the lab in
the cooler the same morning.

## Why we tested
- Bought the place in 2023 and the seller's test was from 2019.
- The water smelled a bit like rotten eggs in July, and the goats were drinking less.
- Extension office recommends testing private wells once a year, and after any flooding. The creek
  came up over the lower pasture in May.

## Summary from the lab
The lab's cover letter says the water is "acceptable for household use with treatment". Main
points they flagged:
1. Total coliform bacteria present. E. coli absent. They recommend shock chlorination of the well
   and a retest.
2. Iron above the secondary standard (staining, metallic taste; not a health limit).
3. Hardness high. Scale on the kettle and the water heater element.
4. pH a little low (slightly acidic water). Can corrode copper pipes over time.

## What the panel covered
Standard Rural Panel: total coliform, E. coli, pH, hardness, iron, manganese, nitrate, nitrite,
chloride, sulfate, copper, total dissolved solids. Metals beyond iron, manganese and copper need
the Extended Metals Panel, which I did not order this time ($85 more).

## Bacteria
- Total coliform: PRESENT
- E. coli: ABSENT

The lab note says coliform usually means surface water getting in somewhere: a cracked well cap, a
bad casing seal, or runoff pooling at the wellhead. Things to check:
- [x] Well cap: the old one had a gap at the conduit. Replaced with a sanitary cap 2026-09-06.
- [x] Regraded the soil so water runs away from the wellhead.
- [ ] Ask about the casing seal next time a well company is out here.
- [x] Shock chlorinate (done 2026-11-03, see [[Journal 2026-11-03]]).
- [ ] Retest bacteria after shocking (the lab says wait until there is no chlorine smell at all,
  usually one to two weeks).

## Treatment ideas
Talked it over with June, who had the same iron problem on her place.
- Sediment filter before the pressure tank: cheap, catches the rust flakes.
- Iron filter (air injection) if staining stays bad. Quotes run around $1,200 installed.
- Water softener for the hardness: maybe later. Goats don't care about hardness.
- UV light for bacteria if the retest is still positive. Would rather fix the source first.
- Neutralizer for the low pH if the copper numbers go up next year.

For the animals: the vet said the iron and hardness are not a problem for goats or chickens at these
levels. The coliform is a bigger concern for the kids in spring, so the retest matters before
kidding.
