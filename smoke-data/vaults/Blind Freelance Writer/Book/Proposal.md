# Proposal — *Rented Lives*

Agent: [[Maeve Doran]], Halcyon Literary. Schedule in [[Timeline]] (book). Chapters in [[Outline]] (book).

## Overview
*Rented Lives* is a book about the people who rent in Dublin: who they are, what they pay, what they give up, and
how a city came to depend on a generation that cannot settle. It is built from ten years of my reporting on housing
for The Dublin Ledger, Harbour Weekly and Atlantic Current, and from new interviews with tenants, landlords,
councillors and the people who work in between: letting agents, inspectors, the staff of the tenancy board.

It is not a policy book. Each chapter follows one household or one building across a year, and uses their story to
open one part of the system: deposits, the register, the zones, evictions for sale, student beds, the long commute.

## Why now
Renting used to be a stage. For most people under forty in Dublin it is now the whole of adult life. There are
books about the housing crisis written by economists and by politicians; there is no book that sits in the kitchen
with the people living it.

## The author
Orla Keane has been a freelance journalist in Dublin for eleven years. Her housing reporting has run in The Dublin
Ledger, Harbour Weekly and Atlantic Current. She has rented eleven homes in the city and currently rents a
one-bedroom flat in Dublin 7.

## Comparable titles
- A long-form book on one American city's evictions, built around eight families (a model for structure)
- An oral history of a London tower block (a model for voice)
- A collection of essays on Irish emigration (shows the Irish market for narrative non-fiction)

## Sample material
The proposal goes out with two sample chapters, about 12,000 words between them: chapter 1 (the deposit) and
chapter 4 (the zones). Chapter 4 draws on the rent pressure zones piece for the Ledger.

## Marketing
- Existing readership across three outlets

- Radio: two regular slots on current affairs programmes already
