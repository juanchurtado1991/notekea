# Transcript — Declan Moran, 2026-10-14

Café in Phibsborough, 1h 40m. Recorded with consent. Off the record: nothing here is quoted with his name.
Cleaned-up transcript, my questions as **OK**, his answers as **DM**. Timestamps approximate.

**OK:** How did you start at Greenmoor?
**DM:** Through my brother-in-law, 2021. They needed drivers with the heavy licence. Day work at first, skips and
building sites around north Dublin, then the Cork runs once a fortnight.

**OK:** What was the pay like?
**DM:** Normal enough. Hourly, plus overtime. They paid €25 extra for Saturday shifts, which everyone wanted. Nothing
strange about it the first year.

**OK:** When did that change?
**DM:** Around September 2022. The yard manager asks a few of us if we want night work. Not every night. Three,
four nights a week. Loads from the sites in Finglas and Ballymun, the stuff that should have gone to the licensed
facility, and instead we bring it out to the farm at Portrane.

**OK:** Did you know what was in the loads?
**DM:** Mostly rubble, soil, broken concrete. Some of it smelled. Some of it had plastic through it, cable, that
kind of thing. One load I remember was black, oily. I asked about that one and was told to mind my own business.

**OK:** Who was at the farm when you arrived?
**DM:** A lad with a digger. He'd open the gate on the north side, we'd tip, he'd spread it and cover it by the
morning. You'd never see a tipping mark in daylight.

**OK:** Were there dockets?
**DM:** There were dockets for the day loads. For the night loads there was a different book, a red one, that stayed
in the yard office. I took photos of some pages because I didn't trust them. I'll show you when I'm ready.

**OK:** Did anyone from the council ever come?
**DM:** Not at night. We were told once, I think it was spring 2024, to stop for two weeks. Then it started again.
Somebody said there was an inspection coming and then it wasn't coming.

**OK:** How many of you did the night runs?
**DM:** Four drivers, two lorries. Six or seven tonnes a load, depending.

**OK:** And the weighbridge?
**DM:** Night loads never went over it. That was the point. Paper records for the day loads were kept in the
portacabin, and when they cleared it out in 2025 the lot went in a skip.

**OK:** When did the night runs stop?
**DM:** March 2025. Just stopped. No explanation. A couple of months later I left.

**OK:** Why did you leave?
**DM:** Back trouble, mostly. And I didn't like what I was doing. My kids swim at the beach there.

**OK:** Would you ever go on the record?
**DM:** No. Not while my brother-in-law works there. Maybe never. You can say a former driver.

**OK:** Is there anyone else who'd talk?
**DM:** One of the other drivers moved to Australia. I'll ask him. The other two are still there.

**OK:** Last thing. How were you paid for the night work? Same as the day?
**DM:** No, that's the thing. Not through the payroll at all. €40 cash per extra night load, in an envelope on a
Friday. The yard manager handed them out. That's why I'm sure it was off the books.

**OK:** Thanks, Declan.
