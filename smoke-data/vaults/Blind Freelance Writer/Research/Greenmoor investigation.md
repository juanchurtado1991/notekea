# Greenmoor investigation

Working title: **The Portrane Fill**. Commissioned by [[Siobhan Kerr]], 6,000 words.

The question: did Greenmoor Haulage dump construction and demolition waste, some of it contaminated, on farmland
next to the stream at Portrane between 2022 and 2025, and did the council know?

Related: [[Timeline]] (research), [[Outline]] (research), [[FOI requests]].
Lab results: see [[Transcript - Aoife Brady]], not repeated here.

## What I have

### People
- A former driver ([[Declan Moran]]) who did the night runs. Off the record.
- A former council inspector ([[Peter Cullen]]) who says inspections were postponed. Off the record.
- A scientist ([[Aoife Brady]]) who tested the stream. On the record.
- The landowner, who has not answered three letters. Letters sent 2026-09-18, 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-20.

### Documents
- Planning file for the farm: permission for "land improvement" by infill, 2021, capped at a small volume.
- Company accounts for Greenmoor Haulage, 2021–2024, from the companies registry. Revenue jumps in 2023
  without a matching jump in declared licensed tonnage.
- Two waste collection permits, one of which lapsed in mid-2024 and was renewed late.
- Court records from a 2019 case where the company was fined for a smaller dump in Meath.

### Places
- The Portrane site: a field off the coast road, with a gate on the north side and a stream on the east edge.
- The Greenmoor depot in Cork (visited 2026-10-17): yard, weighbridge, a portacabin office. Saw lorries with the
  Greenmoor livery but no Dublin plates that day.
- The stream: runs into the estuary about 700 metres downstream.

## What I don't have yet
- Anyone at Greenmoor on the record. The press office is a single email address that bounces.
- The council's side. The press office promised an answer "in due course" on 2026-10-30.
- Weighbridge records. Declan Moran says they were kept on paper and "went in a skip" in 2025.
- Aerial photos for 2023. The free map service only has 2021 and 2025 imagery; the difference is obvious but I
  need the in-between year to date it.

## Lines of inquiry
1. Volumes. How much went in? The permission allowed a small infill. The site is now visibly higher along the
   whole north half.
2. Money. Who paid whom? Declan's account of cash for night loads suggests the night runs were off the books.
3. Oversight. Why were two inspections postponed? Who asked? Peter's account plus whatever the FOI turns up.
4. Harm. What's in the stream and did it get worse after 2023? Aoife's results and her second round.
5. History. The Meath case shows a pattern but I need to be careful not to lean on it.

## Rough numbers
The permission allowed infill of about 5,000 tonnes over two years. Counting lorry loads from Declan's account
(roughly four night loads a week for about two and a half years, at around 6 to 7 tonnes a load) and comparing the
2021 and 2025 ground levels, my working estimate is that about 3,400 tonnes beyond the permitted volume went in.
This is the number I'd most like a second method for. Aoife thinks a soil survey could pin it down; it would cost
money Northlight hasn't offered.
