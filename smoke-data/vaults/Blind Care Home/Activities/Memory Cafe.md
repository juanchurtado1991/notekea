# Memory Cafe

Wednesdays 10:00 in the dining room, run by me with volunteer [[Ruth Okafor]]. Open to residents living with
dementia and to their families, who are welcome to drop in for the hour.

## Why we run it
The cafe gives residents a calm, familiar hour that feels like going out for a coffee rather than an "activity".
Families tell us it is the easiest time to visit, because there is something to talk about and nobody has to fill
the silence. Staff from the nursing floor sometimes join for ten minutes, which helps residents see familiar faces
in a relaxed setting.

## How a session runs
1. Tables laid like a real cafe: cloths, small vases, printed menus with large type.
2. Tea, coffee and a choice of biscuits served on proper cups, never beakers unless a care plan says so.
3. A memory box for the week (seaside, school days, wash day, dance halls, the Leeds markets).
4. Gentle conversation prompts; we never quiz people or correct them.
5. A song or two at the end, usually led by Ruth.

## What we have learned
- Keep the group under twelve. Above that it gets loud and people withdraw.
- Edith Rowe responds best to the doll and to the seaside box; she becomes more talkative afterwards.
- Background music should be quiet and without words, otherwise conversation stops.
- Photographs from the residents' own lives work better than generic pictures. Ask families for copies.
- Avoid the last fifteen minutes before lunch: residents start to worry about missing their meal.
- Name badges for volunteers in big letters help more than anything else.

- Print a simple menu card residents can take back to their rooms.
