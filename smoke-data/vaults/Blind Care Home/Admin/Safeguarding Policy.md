# Safeguarding Policy — Willowmere House

This is my working copy of the home's safeguarding policy, as it applies to activities, outings and volunteers.
The signed original is in the manager's office.

## 1. Purpose
Every resident at Willowmere House has the right to live free from abuse, neglect and exploitation. This policy
sets out how staff and volunteers recognise, prevent and respond to concerns. It applies to everyone who works or
volunteers in the home, including visiting performers, instructors and drivers who spend time with residents.

## 2. Roles
- Designated Safeguarding Lead: Denise Ogun, home manager.
- Deputy Safeguarding Lead: Colin Marsh, deputy manager.
- Every member of staff and every volunteer is responsible for raising a concern. Nobody should assume that
  somebody else has already reported it.

## 3. Types of abuse
Staff and volunteers should be aware of physical, sexual, psychological and emotional abuse, financial or material
abuse, neglect and acts of omission, self-neglect, discriminatory abuse, organisational abuse and modern slavery.
Signs can be subtle in residents living with dementia: changes in mood, withdrawal from activities they used to
enjoy, unexplained bruising, fear of a particular person, missing belongings or money, or a sudden change in how a
visitor behaves around them.

## 4. Prevention in activities
- Group activities take place in shared rooms with the door open, unless privacy is needed for personal care.
- One-to-one visits in a resident's bedroom are carried out by staff, not by volunteers, unless the visit is in the
  resident's care plan and the bedroom door stays open.
- Visiting performers and instructors are always accompanied by a member of staff.
- Residents' money is never handled by volunteers. Outing costs are paid from the amenity fund and recorded.
- Residents' preferences about touch (hand massage, hugs, holding hands in songs) are recorded and respected.

## 5. Recognising and responding
If a resident tells you something that worries you:
1. Listen calmly and take it seriously. Do not ask leading questions.
2. Reassure the resident that they have done the right thing in telling you.
3. Do not promise to keep it secret; explain that you will need to tell someone who can help.
4. Make sure the resident is safe now. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 999.
5. Tell the Designated Safeguarding Lead, or the deputy if the lead is not on site, the same day.

## 6. Recording
Every concern, however small, must be recorded on form SG-3 within 24 hours of the concern being raised. Write down
what you saw or heard, in the resident's own words where possible, with the date, time and the names of anyone
present. Sign and date the form and hand it to the Designated Safeguarding Lead. Do not keep copies at home or on a
personal phone. Forms are stored in the locked cabinet in the manager's office.

## 7. Referral
The Designated Safeguarding Lead decides whether the concern is referred to Leeds adult social care and, where a
crime may have been committed, to the police. The lead also decides whether the Care Quality Commission must be
notified. Staff and volunteers who are unhappy with the decision may raise it directly with the local authority.

## 8. Allegations against staff or volunteers
Concerns about a member of staff or a volunteer are reported to the home manager. If the concern is about the home
manager, report it to the regional operations director. The person concerned may be asked to step back from duties
while the matter is looked into. This is not a disciplinary sanction.

## 9. Volunteers
- All volunteers need an enhanced DBS check before their first session, renewed every three years.
- Volunteers complete the [[Volunteer Induction]] and read this policy before leading any activity.
- Volunteers never take residents off site on their own.
- Volunteers do not accept gifts from residents or families beyond small tokens such as a card or chocolates.

## 10. Outings
- A named member of staff leads every outing and carries the list of residents, medication and emergency contacts.
- The staff to resident ratio on outings is at least one to three, and one to one for residents who need a
  wheelchair on uneven ground.
- Head counts are taken when leaving, on arrival and before every move.

## 11. Training
All staff complete safeguarding adults training on induction and a refresher every 18 months. Volunteers attend a
shorter briefing every year. Training is recorded in the [[Training Log]].

## 12. Photographs and social media
Photographs of residents may only be taken on the home's own devices. A photograph of a resident may only be posted
on the home's social media page or newsletter once the resident, or their attorney, has signed consent form PC-2.
Consent can be withdrawn at any time; remove the photo the same day. Never post photographs that show a resident in
distress, in night clothes or receiving personal care.

## 13. Review
This policy is reviewed every year by the Designated Safeguarding Lead. Next review: April 2027.
