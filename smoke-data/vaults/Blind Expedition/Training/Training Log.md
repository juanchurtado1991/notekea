## Bear Peak — 2026-06-07
First real test after the winter. Legs fine on the way up, knees complained on the steep stone steps down
the Fern Canyon side. Lesson: use the poles on the descent, not just the ascent. Ate too little; bonked a bit
at the saddle. Weather perfect, home before the afternoon storms.

## Mount Bierstadt — 2026-06-28
First 14er of the season. Started at 05:30 from Guanella Pass, summit at 08:50. Mild headache above 13000
ft that went away on the way down with water. Slow and steady, never out of breath for long. A good
reminder that I need to drink more than feels natural up high.

## Grays and Torreys — 2026-07-19
Two summits in one day. Saddle traverse was windy and cold even in July. Tried the new boots for the first
time on rock: comfortable, but heavy. Felt the pack weight on the last 500 ft up Torreys. Pace on the
second summit about 20 % slower than on the first — that is the number to watch.

## Longs Peak — 2026-08-15
The big one. Alarm at 01:00, on the trail at 02:15 with [[Alex Moreno]]. Boulder Field at sunrise, Keyhole
at 07:30. The Narrows and the Homestretch were slow because of a crowd and some ice in the shade. Summit
11:05, cutting it close with the clouds building. Down was long; 12h10 car to car. Very tired but no
altitude symptoms at all. Separate note: [[Longs Peak]].

## Mount Elbert — 2026-09-20
Highest point in Colorado, 4401 m. Went with [[Priya Raman]] to test her pace before the trip; she was
stronger than me on the steep last section. Carried 12 kg to simulate the Mera pack. Cold wind on top.
Separate note: [[Mount Elbert]].

## Pikes Peak — 2026-10-18
Crags route, with [[Alex Kowalski]]. My watch died at the start and I forgot to note the time when we
topped out, so I have no time for this one. Snow from about 12500 ft, used microspikes. Heaviest pack so
far, 13 kg. Felt strong. Separate note: [[Pikes Peak]].

## What I have learned so far
- Above 13000 ft my pace drops by about a quarter no matter how fit I am. Plan for it on Mera.
- Eat every hour, even when not hungry.
- Poles on every descent. Knees thank me.
- The boots need more break-in time; did two long walks around Boulder in them in October.
- Hydration: aim for 4 L on long days, more up high.

## Oxygen readings
I took the finger pulse oximeter on the last three climbs to see how I react to altitude.
- Longs Peak summit (14259 ft): 89 %, pulse 104.
- Mount Elbert summit: SpO2 86% after ten minutes sitting, pulse 98. Lowest reading so far.
- Pikes Peak summit: 90 %, pulse 101.
For comparison my resting value in Boulder is in [[Medical Checks]].
