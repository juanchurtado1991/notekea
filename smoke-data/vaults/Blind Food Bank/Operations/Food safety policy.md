# Food safety policy

This policy applies to every staff member and volunteer who receives, stores, sorts or hands out
food at Tasker Street Community Pantry, at the main site and on the mobile pantry. Read it during
[[Orientation]] and again whenever it changes. The operations manager owns this document.

## 1. Purpose

We serve people who often have no margin for a lost day of work or a sick child. Food we give out
has to be at least as safe as food from a store. When in doubt, throw it out, and write down what
was thrown out and why.

## 2. Personal hygiene

- Wash hands before every shift, after breaks, after handling trash and after touching raw meat
  packaging.
- Gloves are required for any unpackaged food, including bread from the bakery racks.
- Hair restraints (hairnets or caps) are required on the sort line and in the walk-in units.
- Anyone with vomiting, diarrhea or fever in the last 24 hours does not work a food shift. They can
  do office work or come back another day; nobody gets in trouble for staying home.
- Cuts on hands are covered with a bandage and a glove.

## 3. Receiving donations

- Every load is weighed on the dock scale and entered in the donation log the same day.
- Cold food (dairy, deli, fresh meat) must arrive at 41°F or colder. Probe one item from the middle
  of the load, not the top.
- Frozen food must arrive frozen solid, at 0°F or colder at the surface.
- A load that fails the temperature check is refused. If it is already off the truck it is
  discarded, logged as rejected with the temperature, and never counted toward donated pounds.
- Hot prepared food is only accepted from licensed kitchens and must arrive at 135°F or above.
- Home-canned and home-cooked food is never accepted, however good it looks.
- Baby formula and baby food are accepted only before the use-by date.

## 4. Dates and labels

- "Best by" and "sell by" dates are quality dates, not safety dates. Shelf-stable food may be
  given out after them according to the date guide posted by the sort line.
- "Use by" dates on dairy and meat are followed strictly.
- Every case that goes on the shelves gets the receiving date written on it in marker.
- Repackaged bulk food (rice, beans, oats) is labeled with the product name, the date it was
  repacked and the allergen statement copied from the original bag.

## 5. Cans and packaging

- Cans with a dent on the top or bottom seam, a bulge, rust that does not wipe off, or a leak are
  discarded.
- Small dents on the body of the can, away from the seams, are fine.
- Boxes with torn inner bags, glass jars with a raised lid, and anything with signs of pests are
  discarded.
- Cases set aside for inspection are marked ON HOLD and are not handed out until the operations
  manager clears them.

## 6. Storage

- Dry storage: 6 inches off the floor, 18 inches from the ceiling, first in first out.
- Walk-in cooler: 41°F or colder. Walk-in freezer: 0°F or colder.
- Temperatures are read and written in the freezer log at opening and at closing every day the
  building is open.
- Raw meat is stored on the bottom shelves, below ready-to-eat food.
- Cleaning chemicals are stored in the janitor closet only, never in the warehouse aisles.

## 7. Allergens

- Allergen signs go out at every distribution.
- Peanut butter and tree nut products are shelved in their own bay in aisle B.
- Volunteers never tell a client a food is "safe" for an allergy; they show them the label.

## 8. Recalls

- The operations manager checks recall notices every Monday.
- Any product under recall is pulled, marked ON HOLD with the lot number, and kept apart until the
  donor or the manufacturer says how to dispose of it.
- If recalled food already went out, we post a notice at the next distribution and on the mobile
  pantry van.

## 9. Temperature excursions

- If a walk-in reading is out of range, check it again after 15 minutes with the backup
  thermometer.
- If it is still out of range, call the operations manager and move the product to the other unit
  or to the freezer trailer.
- Write every excursion in the freezer log with the time, the reading and what was done.

## 10. Thawing and holding

- Frozen meat is thawed only in the walk-in cooler, never on the counter or in water.
- Thawed meat may be held in the walk-in cooler for 48 hours at most; after that it is discarded,
  not refrozen.
- Bread does not go in the freezer unless the operations manager says there is space.

## 11. Discards

- Small discards go in the compost or trash right away and are noted on the sort sheet.
- Any discard over 100 pounds in one go needs sign-off from the operations manager, Walt
  Brzezinski, before it leaves the building, so the reason can go in the donor's record.

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed every March. Volunteers can suggest changes to the volunteer coordinator at
any time.
