# Grant report draft — Schuylkill Hunger Relief Fund

Working draft for the annual report. Numbers through September are final; October and November
get filled in once Priya closes the month.

## 1. Organization summary

Tasker Street Community Pantry has served South Philadelphia since 2011. We run a Saturday
distribution at our main site on Tasker Street, a three-stop mobile pantry, and a small senior box
program in partnership with two building managers. We are run almost entirely by volunteers, with
one paid operations manager and a part-time volunteer coordinator.

This year the fund's support let us lease a refrigerated van, which changed what we can accept
from grocery partners. Before the van, about a third of the dairy and frozen offers we got had to
be turned down because we could not keep them cold on the way back. Since the lease started in
April we have turned down almost nothing for that reason.

## 2. People served

From January to September we served 6,412 households at the main site, unduplicated by quarter
as the fund asks. The mobile pantry added 1,388 household visits across Fishtown, Hunting Park and
Eastwick. Many of the mobile pantry households are seniors who cannot stand in the Saturday line.

We saw the steepest growth in late summer, when school was out and SNAP changes hit families with
children. The Saturday line now regularly runs past the corner and we are adding a second intake
table, an idea that came from one of our intake volunteers.

## 3. Food distributed

We distributed 352,800 pounds of food from January through September. About 41 percent was fresh
produce, which is the share we most want to grow. Bakery and dairy together were about 22 percent.
Canned and dry goods made up the rest, mostly from parish and school food drives.

We follow a written food safety policy for receiving, storage and discards. Loads that arrive out
of temperature are refused and logged, and they never count toward the pounds we report.

## 4. Volunteers

Volunteers gave 2,914 hours from January to September. Individual volunteers and group volunteers
(schools, companies) are tracked separately, as the fund asks. We ran three new volunteer
orientations over the summer and kept most of the people who came.

Two volunteers deserve a special mention: our Saturday warehouse lead, who trained every new sorter
this year, and our Tuesday data volunteer, who built the household count template this report
relies on.

## 5. Client stories

Story one (permission on file): a grandmother raising two grandchildren in Eastwick who uses the
mobile pantry every Thursday and says the fresh vegetables are the only ones the kids get all week.

Story two (permission still to get): a line cook who lost hours in the spring and came to the
Saturday distribution for three months, and now volunteers on the sort line.

## 6. Challenges

- The walk-in freezer had a temperature excursion in October; product was moved in time and the
  compressor was serviced.
- Bread runs out by late morning on most Saturdays.
- We still need a chest freezer for overflow; we are waiting on quotes.

## 7. Next year

We plan to add a fourth mobile pantry stop, extend Saturday hours during the holidays, and grow the
share of fresh produce with two more farm partners.
