# Water audit 2026

Audit of the community water system of the ASADA de San Isidro de Grecia, March to August 2026. Prepared by Ing. Carolina Mora Vindas, HidroConsulta, and delivered to the board on 15 September 2026. Discussed at the [[2026-10-29 ASADA assembly]].

## 1. Summary

The ASADA de San Isidro serves 1,140 connections, about 3,900 people, from two springs and one well. Over the six months of the audit the water met the free chlorine target at 10 of the 14 sampling points. The four points below the target are all at the far ends of the network. E. coli was found twice, at P-11 in June and at P-13 in August, both at points with low chlorine. Turbidity stayed under the limit everywhere, but was highest at P-09, at the end of the El Cedro line.

Non-revenue water, the water produced but not billed, was 31 % over the six months, mostly lost through leaks in the old PVC of the El Cedro line and through the 86 connections that have no meter.

The audit makes seven recommendations (section 7). R1, replacing the chlorinator at Tanque Alto, is the most urgent and should be done first.

## 2. Scope and method

Water was sampled at 14 points (Annex A) in three rounds: 3 March, 9 June and 28 August 2026. Each sample was tested for free chlorine and pH on site, and for turbidity and E. coli at the Laboratorio Nacional de Aguas within six hours. The results in section 4 are the averages of the three rounds; the E. coli results are given round by round in Annex A.

Production was read monthly at the outlets of the three tanks, and billed water was taken from the ASADA's billing records, from March to August.

The audit did not measure metals, pesticides or nitrates, which were outside its scope. The last analysis of those, by the regional laboratory in 2024, is attached to the ASADA's records and was not repeated.

## 3. The system

### 3.1 Sources

| Source | Type | Flow (L/s) | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naciente La Palma | spring | 6.2 | Tanque Alto |
| Naciente El Cedro | spring | 4.1 | Tanque Alto |
| Pozo 2 | well | 3.5 | Tanque Bajo |

The two springs are protected by concrete boxes in good condition. Pozo 2 has no fence, and cattle from the neighbouring farm graze within 20 m of the well head.

### 3.2 Storage and treatment

Water is stored in three tanks: Tanque Alto, of 250 m³, built in 1998; Tanque Bajo, of 120 m³, built in 2009; and Tanque Los Ángeles, of 60 m³, built in 2019. Chlorine is added only at Tanque Alto and Tanque Bajo, with drip chlorinators. Tanque Los Ángeles receives water already chlorinated from Tanque Alto and adds none.

### 3.3 Network

The network has 38 km of pipe. Eleven kilometres are PVC laid more than 30 years ago, three of them on the El Cedro line. There are 1,140 connections, of which 86 have no meter and pay a flat rate.

The chlorine at Tanque Alto's outlet varied widely between rounds, from 0.31 to 0.82 mg/L, which points to a chlorinator that doses unevenly. Chlorine falls along the network and is lowest where the pipes are longest and the water stands longest: P-09, P-11, P-13 and P-14.

## 5. Findings

| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The chlorinator at Tanque Alto doses unevenly | High |
| F2 | Free chlorine is below the target at four points at the ends of the network | High |
| F3 | The old PVC of the El Cedro line leaks often | High |
| F4 | 86 connections have no meter | Medium |
| F5 | Pozo 2 has no fence, and cattle graze near it | Medium |
| F6 | The tariff has not been reviewed since 2021 | Medium |
| F7 | The plumber has had no training in disinfection or sampling | Low |
| F8 | Production and repairs are recorded only on paper | Low |

F1 and F2 are linked: a steady dose at Tanque Alto and a second dosing point at Tanque Los Ángeles would raise the chlorine at the far points. E. coli appeared only where chlorine was low, which makes F1 and F2 the findings with most bearing on health.

Non-revenue water was 31 % of production over the six months, against 20 % or less in well-run rural systems of the same size. The leaks repaired in the period (Annex B) were mostly on the El Cedro line; the rest of the loss is water used at the unmetered connections and leaks not yet found.

## 7. Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Cost (USD) | Deadline | Responsible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Replace the chlorinator at Tanque Alto | 2,800 | 31 December 2026 | Board |
| R2 | Replace the 3 km of old PVC on the El Cedro line, in two phases | 41,000 | 30 June 2028 | Board |
| R3 | Install meters at the 86 unmetered connections | 6,450 | 31 March 2027 | Plumber |
| R4 | Add a second chlorination point at Tanque Los Ángeles | 3,900 | 30 April 2027 | Board |
| R5 | Fence Pozo 2 and keep cattle 50 m away | 1,200 | 28 February 2027 | Board |
| R6 | Train the plumber in disinfection and sampling | 350 | 31 January 2027 | Regional office |
| R7 | Review the tariff with the regulator | 0 | 30 June 2027 | Treasurer |

R1 is the most urgent: until it is done, the dose that leaves Tanque Alto cannot be trusted, and no other measure on chlorine will hold. R2 is the largest cost; the ASADA should seek a grant from the regional office for its second phase.

## 8. Conclusions

The water of San Isidro is safe at most points most of the time, but not at the ends of the network, where low chlorine has already let E. coli through twice this year. The causes are known and the remedies are not expensive, except the pipe of the El Cedro line. The audit recommends that the board report on the progress of each recommendation at every general assembly until all seven are done.
