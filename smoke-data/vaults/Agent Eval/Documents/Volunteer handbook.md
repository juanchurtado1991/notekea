# Volunteer handbook

The handbook every new volunteer of Patitas Animal Shelter receives at orientation, 2026 edition. Copied from the shelter's shared folder so I have it offline. [[Laura Méndez]] volunteers here too.

## 1. Welcome

Thank you for giving your time to the animals of Patitas. Volunteers walk, feed, clean, socialize and drive for the animals, and they are the reason most of our dogs and cats find a home. This handbook tells you how the shelter works, what you may and may not do, and who to ask. Read it before your first shift and keep it at hand; the rules in it apply to every volunteer, whatever their age or experience.

## 2. About Patitas

Patitas was founded in 2014 by Ofelia Arguedas in a borrowed coffee shed and moved to its present site, 2 km east of the centre of Grecia, in 2018. It has room for 120 dogs and 45 cats. Six paid staff run the shelter, and about 70 volunteers are active in any given month. Every animal is vaccinated, dewormed and sterilized before adoption. Patitas never puts down a healthy animal to make room.

## 3. Becoming a volunteer

Volunteers must be at least 16 years old. A volunteer under 18 needs a consent form (form F-1) signed by a parent or guardian, and must work alongside an adult volunteer on every shift.

Every new volunteer attends an orientation of 3 hours, held on the first Saturday of each month at 9:00 in the classroom next to reception. After the orientation you work 4 supervised shifts with an experienced volunteer; this is your trial period. At the end of it the volunteer coordinator confirms you as a volunteer.

Before your first shift you must show proof of a tetanus vaccine given within the last 10 years.

## 4. Shifts

There are three shifts:

- Morning, 7:00 to 11:00, every day.
- Afternoon, 13:00 to 17:00, every day.
- Evening, 17:00 to 20:00, on weekdays only.

Each volunteer commits to at least 2 shifts a month. Sign up for the coming week on the shared calendar by Thursday at 18:00. A shift with fewer than three volunteers signed up by Friday is announced in the volunteers' group.

## 5. Signing in and out

Sign in and out in the logbook at reception on every shift, with the time you arrived and left. Your hours in the logbook are the only hours that count for recognition (section 16), so do not forget them. The shift lead is the staff member named on the board behind reception; ask them first about anything during the shift.

## 6. Dog walking

Every dog wears a collar tag in one of three colours, and every volunteer has a walking level that says which dogs they may take out.

- Green level: given to every volunteer after orientation. Green walkers may take out dogs with a green collar tag.
- Yellow level: given after a volunteer has done 10 walks and taken the 45-minute leash-handling course, which runs on the third Saturday of each month at 10:00. Both are needed. Yellow walkers may take out dogs with a green or yellow collar tag.
- Red level: staff only. Dogs with a red tag are not walked by volunteers.

Walks last 20 to 30 minutes, on the marked trail behind the kennels. Never let two dogs greet each other on a walk, never take a dog off the lead, and bring the dog back at once if it pulls hard or shows fear. The kennel zones and their levels are in Annex A.

## 7. Cat rooms

No more than 3 volunteers may be in a cat room at the same time. Wash your hands when you enter and leave each room, so that illness does not pass from room to room. Shoes stay outside the kitten room. Sit on the floor and let the cats come to you; do not pick up a cat that hides.

## 8. Feeding

Dogs are fed at 8:00 and 16:00. Cats are fed at 7:30, 12:30 and 17:30. Use only the food prepared in the kitchen, in the bowl marked with the animal's kennel number. Treats may be given only from the blue jar at reception, no more than two per animal per shift. Never feed an animal with a red dot on its card; it is on a special diet.

## 9. Health and medication

Only the vet tech, Ana Lucía Brenes, gives medication. Volunteers never give pills, drops or injections, even when an animal's card says what it takes. Tell the shift lead at once if an animal does not eat, vomits, has diarrhoea, limps, coughs or seems unusually quiet.

## 10. Safety and bites

Report any bite or scratch to the shift lead within one hour, however small, and fill in the incident form F-3 before you leave. The first aid kit is at reception. A bite that breaks the skin must be seen at the clinic in Grecia the same day; the shelter pays the visit. A dog that has bitten is moved to the red level until the staff have assessed it.

## 11. Dress and belongings

Wear closed shoes on every shift, and long trousers for dog walks. Do not wear rings, bracelets or long necklaces. Lockers are next to the classroom; bring your own padlock. Phones stay in your pocket while you handle an animal.

## 12. Photos and social media

You are welcome to take photos of the animals. Do not show the faces of adopters or visitors. Posts about our animals on your own accounts need the approval of the communications coordinator, Paula Jiménez, so that the information about each animal is right. Never share an animal's medical details.

## 13. Adoption events

Adoption events are held on the second Sunday of each month, from 9:00 to 14:00, in the Parque Central de Grecia. Each event needs 8 volunteers. Set-up starts at 8:00 and the van leaves the shelter at 7:30. Only yellow-level volunteers handle dogs at the events; green-level volunteers help at the tables, with the cats and with the forms.

## 14. Fostering

Foster homes take animals that are too young, too old or too nervous for the kennels. The shelter gives each foster home 25 USD of food per animal per month and pays every visit to the vet. A home may foster no more than 2 animals at a time. Fosters bring their animals to the adoption events, and sign form F-5 before taking an animal home.

## 15. Transport volunteers

Transport volunteers drive animals to the vet, to foster homes and to adoption events in their own car. They must be over 21 and have held a driving licence for at least 2 years. The shelter pays 0.30 USD per km driven for the shelter, up to 60 USD a month. Send your claim with form F-7 by the 5th of the following month, with the date, route and distance of each trip.

## 16. Recognition

Your hours in the logbook earn:

- 100 hours: the shelter's T-shirt.
- 250 hours: a plaque with your name on a kennel.
- 500 hours: a seat as our guest at the annual dinner in December.
- 1,000 hours: honorary membership of Patitas.

## 17. Absences and leaving

If you cannot come to a shift you signed up for, tell the volunteer coordinator at least 24 hours before. A volunteer who misses three shifts in a quarter without notice is suspended for one month. A volunteer who has been away for more than 6 months repeats the orientation before coming back. If you decide to leave, tell the coordinator and return your locker key.
