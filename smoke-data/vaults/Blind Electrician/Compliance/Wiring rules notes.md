# Wiring rules notes

My own summary from the refresher course I did in October. This is in my words, written for quick reminders on
site. When in doubt, check the actual standard and the regulator's guidance, not this note.

## General approach
- Every installation is designed so that it is safe in normal use and when something goes wrong. Think about the
  fault case first: where does the fault current go, how fast does the protection operate, who can touch what.
- Keep a record of the design decisions on bigger jobs. If an inspector asks why a cable size was chosen, the
  answer should be in the job note, not in my head.
- Do not rely on what was there before. Old work that "has always been fine" is not evidence of anything.

## Protection
- RCD protection on all final circuits for new work and for any circuit I touch in an alteration. The trainer was
  very clear that "only the circuits I added" is not good enough when the board is replaced.
- Use RCBOs where practical so one fault does not take out the whole house. Customers notice when the fridge goes
  off because the outside light got wet.
- Check the rating of the protective device against the cable, not against what the customer wants to plug in.
- Surge protection is worth recommending on any board replacement, especially in areas with a lot of storms.

## Cables and installation
- Derate for grouping and for insulation. Cable buried in ceiling batts runs hotter than the same cable clipped to
  a joist. The course spent a whole morning on this; most callbacks they see are from undersized cable in roofs.
- Voltage drop: keep it within the limit for the whole run from the point of supply. Long rural runs (sheds,
  pumps, granny flats) need checking with the real length, not a guess.
- Underground cable: in conduit, at the right depth, with marker tape above. Note the route on a sketch for the
  customer so nobody digs through it later.
- Keep clearances from gas, water and other services. Write down anything unusual in the job note.
- Mechanical protection where cables could be damaged, like low on garage walls or behind shelving.

## Wet areas
- Bathrooms, laundries and outdoor areas have zones. Check the zone before choosing the fitting and the location of
  every switch and outlet.
- Outdoor outlets need the right IP rating and a weatherproof cover that still closes with a plug in it.
- Pool and spa work needs extra care with equipotential bonding. I do not take pool jobs without reading up first.

## Earthing
- Main earth conductor size follows the main supply. Check the electrode and the connection at the stake on every
  board change; corroded clamps are common near the coast.
- Test the earth continuity on every circuit, including the light fittings, not only the power points.
- Water pipe bonding where required. Old houses sometimes have the earth on the water pipe only, which is not
  acceptable any more if the pipe has been replaced with plastic somewhere.

## Testing before energising
1. Visual inspection, every connection tight, every cover on.
2. Earth continuity.
3. Insulation resistance.
4. Polarity.
5. Correct circuit connections.
6. Earth fault loop impedance.
7. RCD operation, both the trip time and the push button.

Write the results on the test sheet for the job. Keep the sheets with the certificate copies.

## Handover to the customer
- Explain the RCDs and show them the test button. Most people have never pressed it.
- Leave the circuit chart filled in and readable, inside the board door.
- Leave contact details on a sticker inside the board.
- Tell them how to reset a tripped RCBO and when to call instead.

## Things the trainer said to remember
- Photos of every rough-in before the plaster goes on. They save arguments.
- Never leave a job with a temporary fix that is not written down and labelled.
- Hand the customer a reminder to test their RCDs every six months. I now stick an orange "TEST ME" sticker next to
  the RCDs on every board I install or replace, with the date of the install written on it. Greg
  Holt got one on the shed sub-board; the strata blocks get one in each meter room.
