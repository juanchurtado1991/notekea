# Exemption guide (my notes)

My own summary of how the home education exemption works, written when we applied for Isla and Finn
and updated when we did Ruby's. This is from the Ministry's guidance and from what other co-op
parents told me, so check the official pages before relying on any of it. Our granted certificates
are in [[Exemption certificates]].

## 1. Who needs an exemption
Every child must be enrolled at a registered school from their sixth birthday until they turn
sixteen. If you want to teach them at home instead, you need an exemption certificate for each child
before they would otherwise have to be enrolled. A child who has never been to school can be taught at
home before six without one, but you should apply a few months before the sixth birthday because
the assessment takes time. A child who is already at school stays enrolled until the certificate is
granted; you cannot just take them out and apply later.

## 2. Applying
The application is a long form plus a written description of how you will teach. The Ministry wants
to be satisfied that the child will be taught "at least as regularly and well as in a registered
school". In practice that means you need to explain:
- what you will teach across the main learning areas (English, maths, science, social sciences,
  the arts, health and PE, technology, languages);
- how you will teach it: resources, routines, how often, who teaches what;
- how you will know the child is making progress (records, samples of work, tests);
- for a child with extra learning needs, how those needs will be met.
You do not have to follow the national curriculum, but you do have to show a plan that is broad and
balanced. Co-op parents said that a sample week and a list of resources helped a lot.

## 3. What happens after you apply
The Ministry reads the application and may ask for more detail. For us they asked one follow-up
question for Isla (how we would teach science without a lab) and none for Finn. If they are not
satisfied they can decline, and you can apply again with more information. Expect the whole thing to
take six to ten weeks. Keep a copy of everything you send.

## 4. Once the certificate is granted
- The certificate stays in force until the child turns sixteen, unless it is revoked.
- It can be revoked if the Ministry is no longer satisfied the child is being taught as regularly
  and as well as in a registered school. A review can be triggered by a complaint or at random.
- If the child goes back to school, the certificate simply stops applying. Getting a new one later
  means a new application.
- You must teach the child yourself or arrange the teaching (tutors, co-op, online courses are all
  fine). Using an online school part-time does not affect the exemption as long as the child is not
  enrolled there full-time.

## 5. Statements of supervision
Twice a year the Ministry sends a statement for each exempt child, in May and November. You sign it
to confirm that you are still home educating the child and return it by the date on the letter. If
you do not return it, the supervision allowance is not paid and they may follow up about the
exemption. Our November statements usually arrive in the first week of the month.

## 6. Reviews
The Education Review Office can review a home education situation. It is rare. If it happens they
look at your records, talk to you and sometimes to the child, and look at samples of work. Keep:
- a record of what each child did (my progress notes per child),
- some work samples per term,
- the test results we have.
Nobody I know in the co-op has been reviewed in the last five years.

## 7. Supervision allowance
Parents of exempt children get a supervision allowance to help with costs. It is paid in two
instalments a year, in June and December, after the statements of supervision are returned. The
amount depends on how many exempt children are in the family. The rates we get in 2026:
- first child (Isla): $760 a year
- second child (Finn): $640 a year
- third child (Ruby): $520 a year
- each further child: $400 a year
The allowance is paid to one parent, into the bank account given on the form. It is not taxed as
income. We put it all into the household homeschool budget ([[Admin/Budget]]).

## 8. Changes you must tell them about
Tell the Ministry within 30 days if:
- you move house (new address for each exempt child),
- a child is enrolled at a school, even part-time,
- the person responsible for the teaching changes,
- a child moves overseas.
Missing this can delay the allowance. When we moved from Hamilton to Matangi in 2023 I emailed them
the week after and that was all it took.

## 9. My checklist each year
- [x] May statements signed and returned (2026-05-12)
- [ ] November statements signed and returned
- [ ] Update progress notes for all three before the end of term
- [ ] File this year's work samples in the folder in the hall cupboard
- [ ] Check allowance arrived in December
