## Weekly reflections

### Week 1 (Oct 5–11)
Three runs, all run/walk or very easy. The knee was quiet. I kept the daily isometrics from the
[[Home Exercises]] list and did them before every run: 5 x 45 s wall sits at 60 degrees. The biggest challenge was
mental — I wanted to do more. Mike Brenner reminded me that the first month is about tissue tolerance, not fitness.
Sleep was decent and I noticed my resting heart rate creeping down again.

### Week 2 (Oct 12–18)
Up to three runs of 3 miles or more. Physio on the 14th went well: single-leg press up to 60 kg for 4 x 8 with no
pain. The ache on the 16th was after stairs at school, not during the run, so we counted it as fine. I started
wearing the new road shoes for every easy run and kept the trail shoes for weekends.

### Week 3 (Oct 19–25)
First run over 4 miles. Mike Osei came along on the 19th and kept me honest on the pace — he says my cadence drops
on the downhills. Pain stayed between 0 and 2 all week. Started the collagen-before-rehab routine from
[[Supplements]]. Weekly volume felt sustainable and I slept well.

### Week 4 (Oct 26–31)
Five miles on the 26th, then I got greedy: hill repeats on the 29th (4 x 90 s up the 17th Avenue hill, jog down).
The next morning the knee was 4/10 and stiff on the stairs. I took two days of easy cross-training on the bike and
ran only 2 flat miles on Halloween.

At the physio check on 2026-10-28 the hop tests had looked good, which is probably why I felt ready. After I told
her about the hill session, Sarah Whitlock was clear: no hill repeats and no downhill running until November 16,
then we reintroduce gentle hills one session per week. She also wants the long run to grow by no more than one mile
per week.

### Month notes
- Shoes: road shoes for weekdays, trail shoes for weekends.
- Biggest lesson: one greedy session cost me almost a week.
- Next month: build to a 6-mile long run, keep strength twice a week, respect the hill ban.
