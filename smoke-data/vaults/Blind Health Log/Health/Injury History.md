# Injury History

## Right knee — patellar tendinopathy (2026)

### What happened
On 2026-08-02 I was doing a 14-mile long run on the Highline Canal trail. Around mile 9 I felt a sharp, burning
pain just below the right kneecap. I tried to keep going for another half mile, then walked and finally called my
sister for a ride home. That evening the knee was swollen and stairs were painful.

The weeks before, I had increased my mileage fast for the autumn half (from 28 to 41 miles per week in three
weeks) and done two hill sessions per week. In hindsight that was the cause: too much, too soon, and too much
downhill.

### Diagnosis
- 2026-08-06: first visit with [[Sarah Delgado]] at Foothills Sports Medicine. Clinical diagnosis: right patellar
  tendinopathy. X-ray normal. She stopped the naproxen I had been taking on my own (stomach upset) and referred me
  to physiotherapy.
- 2026-08-12: first physio assessment with [[Sarah Whitlock]], see [[Physio Sessions]].
- 2026-08-20: MRI at Clear Peak Imaging. Report: thickening of the proximal patellar tendon with mild signal
  change, no tear, no bone bruise. Grade: moderate tendinopathy.

### Timeline of recovery
- August: no running. Isometrics, bike with no resistance, swimming (pull buoy only).
- September: eccentric and heavy slow resistance work, return-to-run test passed on 2026-09-30.
- October: return to running, see [[Training Log - October 2026]]. One setback after hill repeats at the end of
  the month.
- November: building the long run on flat routes.

### What helps
- Isometrics before runs (wall sits, Spanish squats).
- Sleep over 7 hours — the knee is clearly worse after short nights (see [[Sleep Log]]).
- Collagen + vitamin C before rehab sessions.
- Ice for 10 minutes after long runs.

### What hurts
- Downhills and stairs.
- Sitting for long periods with the knee bent (grading papers!).
- Deep squats without warming up.

### Older injuries
- 2019: left ankle sprain playing soccer, six weeks out.
- 2023: plantar fasciitis, right foot, resolved with calf work and new shoes in about three months.
- 2025: tight calves and a cramp at mile 22 of the Front Range Marathon; not an injury, but a warning about
  fueling and calf strength.

### Questions I keep asking
- Will the knee handle a marathon in May? Both Sarahs think yes, if the build is gradual.
- Should I change my running form? Mike Brenner thinks a slightly higher cadence will reduce load on the knee.
- Is surgery ever needed? Very rarely for this kind of tendinopathy.

### Plan agreed with Dr. Delgado (follow-up 2026-10-01)
Surgery is not recommended. Keep the loading programme with the physio for at least twelve weeks. If the tendon is
not clearly better on the ultrasound recheck, she offered a platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injection as the next step.
I have to decide about the PRP injection by 2027-02-01 at the latest, so it does not collide with the marathon
build. The injection would mean two weeks without running.
