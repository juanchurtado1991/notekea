# Dobsonian Manual

Coyote Optics CD series Dobsonian telescopes: CD-8, CD-10 and CD-12. Owner's manual, my typed copy
for the [[Dobsonian CD-10]].

## 1. Unpacking

The telescope ships in two boxes. Box one holds the optical tube with the primary mirror already
installed in its cell, the secondary mirror on its spider, and the focuser. Box two holds the rocker
base panels, the ground board, the hardware bag and the accessory tray. Keep the foam inserts: they
are the safest way to move the tube if you ever ship it. Check that the hardware bag contains the
eight base screws, the three ground board feet, the azimuth pivot bolt with its washers, and the
two side bearing knobs.

## 2. Assembling the base

Assemble the rocker box on a flat floor. Fasten the two side panels to the front board with the
base screws, then attach the top board. Do not fully tighten the screws until all panels are in
place, or the box will not square. Fit the ground board feet, then join the ground board to the
rocker box with the azimuth pivot bolt. The pivot should be snug but must let the base turn with a
light push. If the base turns too freely, add the second washer; if it binds, remove it.

## 3. Mounting the tube

Lower the tube into the rocker box with the side bearings resting on the altitude pads. Thread the
side bearing knobs into the tube through the side panels. The knobs set the friction for up and
down motion; tighten them just enough that the tube stays put when you change eyepieces. A heavy
eyepiece or a camera will need more tension.

## 4. The finder

Install the finder bracket on the two screws near the focuser. Align the finder in daylight on a
distant object such as a pole or a treetop: center it in the main scope with the lowest power
eyepiece, then adjust the finder until it points at the same spot. Recheck the alignment on a
bright star at night.

## 5. Collimation

A fast Newtonian must be collimated to perform well. Check collimation before each session,
especially after transport. Use a collimation cap or a laser collimator.

1. Look through the cap. The secondary mirror should appear round and centered under the focuser.
2. Adjust the three secondary screws until the reflection of the primary mirror is centered in the
   secondary.
3. Adjust the primary mirror with the three collimation knobs at the back of the cell until the
   center ring of the primary is centered on the dot of the cap.
4. Lock the primary with the three locking screws, then check again.

A star test at high power confirms the result: a slightly defocused star should show round,
concentric rings.

## 6. Thermal cooling

A mirror warmer than the night air creates currents in the tube that blur the image, especially at
high power. Take the telescope outside before you plan to observe and let it cool. Approximate
times from indoor temperature: the CD-8 needs about 30 minutes, the CD-10 needs 45 minutes and the
CD-12 about an hour. In the desert, where the temperature falls quickly after sunset, plan for
extra time. Do not point the telescope at the Sun while cooling it in daylight.

## 7. Observing tips

Start with the lowest power eyepiece to find an object, then increase power. Most nights the
atmosphere limits useful magnification to around 200x. Use averted vision for faint galaxies and
nebulae: look slightly to one side of the object. Allow 20 minutes for your eyes to adapt to the
dark and use only red light. The open tube design means stray light can enter: a light shield
around the top of the tube helps at sites with nearby lights.

## 8. Transport

Remove the tube from the base before moving. Carry the tube with both hands, never by the focuser
or the finder. In a car, lay the tube flat on a soft surface with the focuser facing up and wedge
it so it cannot roll. Check collimation on arrival.

## 9. Care and cleaning

Keep the dust caps on when the telescope is not in use. Dew will form on cold nights; let it
evaporate indoors with the caps off before putting them back on. Do not wipe the mirror. Clean the
primary mirror no more than once every two years, and only when the dust is heavy: remove the cell,
rinse the mirror with lukewarm distilled water, soak it with a drop of mild soap, rinse again and
let it stand on edge to dry. Never use paper towels or tissues on the coating.

## 10. Troubleshooting

- Stars look like comets or seagulls: collimation is off, see section 5.
- Image boils at high power: the mirror has not cooled, see section 6.
- The tube drifts down after changing eyepieces: tighten the side bearing knobs.
- The base turns in jerks: clean the azimuth pads and the ground board.
- Nothing visible in the eyepiece: remove the dust cap, check the finder alignment.

## 11. Warranty

The optics carry a five-year warranty and the base and mechanical parts one year, from the date of
purchase. Register within 30 days to activate it. The warranty does not cover damage from dropping
the tube, mirror cleaning mistakes or modifications.
