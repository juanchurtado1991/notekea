# Messier Marathon 2026

The club marathon at Ortiz Ranch on the night of 2026-03-21 into the morning of 2026-03-22. Moon new
two days before, forecast clear, low wind. I used the [[Dobsonian CD-10]] with the 25 mm for finding
and the 10 mm to confirm. Rosa, Tom, both Daves and four other members came out; pizza was on the
club (see [[Expenses 2026]]).

## Setup

Arrived at 17:20 and set up on the south pasture next to Rosa. Collimated with the cap, checked the
Telrad against Polaris, laid out the printed sequence chart on the table under a red light. Put the
thermos of coffee where I could reach it without moving the chair. The plan was the standard
sequence: catch the evening galaxies low in the west first, then work east through the winter
objects, the spring galaxy fields after midnight, the summer globulars before dawn, and the last
few in twilight.

## Dusk (18:50 to 20:00)

The western sky was the hardest part as always. M77 came first, low and small, just a fuzzy star
with a brighter core. M31, M32 and M110 were easy, the Andromeda core obvious even in twilight. M33
took a few minutes of sweeping, very low contrast that early. The western galaxy I was most worried
about was already sinking into the glow over the hills, and I spent a long time on it before moving
on to keep the schedule. M52, M103 and the Perseus clusters went quickly after that.

## Winter objects (20:00 to 22:30)

This is the easy and fun stretch. M45 in the finder, M42 and M43 with the nebula filling the field,
M78 like a small comet. The Gemini and Auriga clusters went one after another: M35, M36, M37, M38,
then M1, which was faint but there. Canis Major and Puppis gave M41, M46, M47, M93 and M50, and M48
in Hydra a little after. M44 and M67 in Cancer finished the stretch at about 22:20. Tom kept calling
out his count across the pasture and we were neck and neck.

## Spring galaxies (22:30 to 02:30)

The Leo triplet minus one (M65 and M66), the Leo I group (M95, M96, M105), then M81 and M82 high in
the north and M97 and M108 near the bowl of the Big Dipper. M109 and M40 were quick. Then the long
slog through Virgo and Coma: M98, M99, M100, M85, M84, M86, M87, M88, M91, M89, M90, M58, M59, M60,
M49, M61. Markarian's Chain helped a lot as a road map. I lost about fifteen minutes confusing M91
with a field galaxy and had to back up to M88. M64, M53, M3, M51 and M101, M102 and M63 went after,
then M104 low in the south. At 02:00 the coffee ran out and Dave Kowalski shared his.

## Summer objects (02:30 to 05:10)

M5 and the Scorpius group: M80, M4, M107, then the Ophiuchus globulars M12, M10, M14, M9, M19 and
M62. Hercules gave M13 and M92. M57 and M56 in Lyra, M27 and M71, then M29 and M39 in Cygnus. The
Sagittarius region took time because everything rose at once: M8, M20, M21, M23, M24, M25, M18, M17,
M16, M28, M22, M69, M70, M54, M55 and M75. Scutum gave M11 and M26. M15 and M2 rose together in the
east by 04:40. M72 and M73 were last of the stretch, M72 just a faint smudge.

## Dawn and the final count

M6 and M7 low over the ranch house were easy in binoculars. Then it was a race against twilight in
the east for the last globular, and I did not win it. The final count, written in the club tally at
05:45: 108 of 110. Missed M74 at dusk, lost in the glow over the western hills, and M30 at dawn, the
sky was already too bright when it cleared the horizon. Tom got 107, Rosa 109. Next year: start on
M74 the moment the sun is down, and set up further east so the ranch house doesn't hide the dawn
horizon.
