# Grant application — Coastal Arts Short Film Fund

Deadline: 2026-12-15, 23:59 Pacific. Awards up to $5,000 for short films by students and emerging filmmakers
in California. Budget in [[Grant/Budget]]. Submitted online, one PDF plus a link to a work sample.

---

## 1. Project summary

*Salt and Static* is a 14-minute drama set over one night in a small harbor town. Nell, a waitress on the
graveyard shift, has listened to the same overnight radio host for six years. One night, from the pay phone
of a laundromat, she calls in. The film is about the people who keep a town company while it sleeps, and
about the courage it takes to be heard by the voice that has kept you company.

The film is my thesis project at Westlake Film Institute. It shoots over three nights in November 2026 with
one dawn pickup day in December, and will be finished in early 2027.

## 2. Director's statement

I grew up in a town where the radio was the only thing awake after midnight. My mother worked nights at a
hospital laundry and listened to the same host every shift; she never called in, but she talked about him
like a neighbor. I wanted to make a film about that kind of closeness, the kind that is real even though it
only goes one way, and about the moment it turns into something that goes both ways.

Visually the film lives in practical light: diner neon, laundromat tubes, the red bulb above a radio booth.
The camera is patient. Sound is the second camera; the radio is always placed in the room, small and warm,
so that when Cal's voice finally comes through the phone line instead of the speaker, the audience feels it.

## 3. Production plan

Pre-production runs through mid-November 2026: casting is complete, locations are secured, and permits are
approved or in process. Principal photography is three days, 20 to 22 November 2026, at a working diner,
a coin laundry and a stage at school dressed as a radio booth. A pickup day at a public pier in early
December covers the final scene at sunrise.

Post-production starts right after the pickup day: editorial through early January, then the sound mix and
the color grade, with a finished film by February 2027. The editor, sound house and colorist are booked.

## 5. Budget summary

The full budget for the film is larger than this request. The school covers insurance, stage B and the
camera loan for the test day. My own savings and family support cover the crew and catering. This request
covers the items that school resources cannot: rentals beyond the school's kit, location fees and permits,
the sound mix, the color grade and festival entry fees. Line items are in the attached budget.

## 6. Festival and distribution plan

The film will premiere in California, as the fund asks of every project it supports, and then go to a short
list of festivals chosen for night stories and new voices; the festival fees line covers those entries.
After the festival run the film will be available free online.

## 7. Matching funds and in-kind support

The fund asks applicants to show support from other sources. In-kind support: the school's stage and camera
loan for the test, the gaffer's own lighting kit, the diner owner's reduced day rate, and the editor's flat
fee. Cash: my own savings and family support, as listed in the production budget.

## 8. Community engagement

The film is about a town that keeps itself company at night, so the first screening should be for that town.
After the premiere I will hold a free community screening at the Westport Public Library, with a talk about
night work and local radio, and I will invite the diner staff, the laundromat attendant and the harbor crew
who helped on the shoot. The library has offered its community room and projector at no cost.

## 9. Fiscal sponsor

Because I apply as an individual, the fund needs a fiscal sponsor to receive the award. My fiscal sponsor is
the Harborlight Arts Collective, a nonprofit that has sponsored student shorts in the region before; their
fee is taken from the award and is already reflected in the budget.
