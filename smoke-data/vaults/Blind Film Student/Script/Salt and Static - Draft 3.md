# Salt and Static — Draft 3

Written by Riley Navarro. Draft 3, after [[Draft 2 notes]]. Shooting draft unless the table read breaks it.

Tone notes for the crew: everything is lit from practicals, the neon of the diner sign, the laundromat
tubes, the red ON AIR bulb. Nobody runs. Cuts are slow until scene 5, where we stay on faces. The radio is
a character: we always know where it is in the room, and the sound mix should place it there, small and
warm, never full range. Wardrobe keeps Nell in the same uniform all night so the wash in scene 4 matters.

---

**1. INT. HARBOR DINER - NIGHT**

A narrow diner with six stools and three booths. Rain on the windows. A small radio on the shelf above the
coffee machines plays a low, warm voice. NELL (late 20s, uniform a size too big) wipes the counter in slow
circles. One customer, an OLD FISHERMAN, reads a damp newspaper.

CAL (V.O.)
(on the radio)
It is two in the morning, and if you are awake with me, you are not alone. That is the whole show.

Nell turns the volume up one notch. The fisherman looks up. She turns it back down.

**2. INT. RADIO BOOTH - NIGHT**

A booth the size of a closet. Foam on the walls, a mug of cold coffee. CAL (40s, cardigan, reading glasses on
his head) leans into the microphone. The phone board has four lines. None of them blink.

CAL
Lines are open. I know you are out there. Somebody always is.

He waits. Nothing. He plays a record and leans back, looking at the dark window of the booth.

**3. EXT. HARBOR DINER - NIGHT**

Nell drags a trash bag to the bins. Through the window behind her, the radio is a faint murmur. She stops,
listens, and mouths the words of Cal's sign-off along with him, the way you know a song.

**4. INT. LAUNDROMAT - NIGHT**

Fluorescent hum. Two machines turning. Nell sits on the folding table in her coat while her uniforms wash.
A pay phone on the wall, the last one on the street. She counts coins in her palm. Three quarters short.
She checks the coin slot of the change machine, finds one, then another under the dryer.

NELL
(to herself)
Okay. Okay.

She dials. Close on the coins dropping into the slot.

**5. INT. RADIO BOOTH - NIGHT**

Line two blinks. Cal stares at it as if it might stop. He picks up.

CAL
You are on the air.

NELL (V.O.)
(filtered)
I wash my uniforms on Thursdays. I have listened to you on Thursdays for six years.

CAL
That is a long time to listen to a man talk about nothing.

NELL (V.O.)
It is not nothing.

They talk. We do not hear all of it; the scene breathes. The record ends and he does not change it.

**6. INT. HARBOR DINER - DAWN**

Grey light. Nell back behind the counter, eyes tired. The radio crackles into the morning show's jingle, and
then, before it, the station ident in Cal's voice, recorded years ago:

CAL (V.O.)
(recorded)
You're listening to KSLT, the overnight voice of Westport.

The bell over the door rings. Cal stands in the doorway, cardigan wet from the rain, holding his reading
glasses. He has never been inside. He sits on the stool farthest from her.

CAL
Coffee. And whatever you recommend on a Thursday.

Nell does not turn around right away. When she does, she is almost smiling.

**7. EXT. WESTPORT PIER - DAWN**

The two of them at the end of the pier, not touching, both holding paper cups. The radio from the diner is
in Nell's coat pocket, playing static between stations. She turns the dial until she finds nothing at all,
and leaves it there.

FADE OUT.
