# Arden 2023 – Kelp recovery after marine heatwaves

## Why it matters for me
Chapter 3 and Chapter 4 of my thesis need a link between urchin numbers and whether kelp comes back. This paper is
the best long-term dataset I have found. Helen likes it; Marco thinks the statistics are fine but the site selection
is biased toward easy dive sites.

## What they did
They followed 24 kelp forest sites along a temperate coastline for ten years, starting the year before a major marine
heatwave. Every summer divers measured kelp canopy cover along fixed transects, counted urchins in quadrats, and
noted whether urchins were exposed (actively grazing in the open) or hiding in cracks. Water temperature came from
loggers at 8 m depth at every site.

## The heatwave year
During the heatwave, canopy cover across all sites fell by 62 % compared with the year before. Bottom temperatures
stayed above 20 °C for 41 days at the warmest sites. Kelp loss was worst at shallow sites and at sites with a lot of
exposed urchins. Sea star wasting disease hit the same region at the same time, so predators of urchins also
collapsed.

## Recovery
This is the core of the paper. After the heatwave, sites split into two clear groups.

Sites that turned into barrens, with urchin densities above 10 per m², showed no recovery at all after 9 years of
monitoring. Some of these sites still had a thin turf of red algae but no kelp recruits that survived past their
first season.

At sites where urchin density stayed below 2 per m², canopy recovered to 80 % of its pre-heatwave cover
within 4 years. Recovery started with understory kelps and then the canopy species returned.

Sites with intermediate densities (between 2 and 10 per m²) were noisy: some recovered slowly, some flipped into
barrens two or three years after the heatwave, when urchins came out of hiding as their food ran out.

## Mechanism they propose
They argue that urchin behaviour is the switch: when drift kelp is plentiful, urchins stay in cracks and eat what
falls to them; when drift runs out, they come out and graze standing kelp, which removes the source of drift and
locks the site into a barren. Temperature acts mostly through the first kelp loss, not through the urchins.

## Weak points
- Only one heatwave, so they cannot separate heat from the wasting disease.
- Site selection biased toward sheltered sites.
- Urchin counts were made in summer only.
