Compound interest explained

By the Example team

Compound interest means earning interest on interest. If you save one thousand euros at five percent a year, you earn fifty euros the first year. The second year you earn five percent of one thousand and fifty euros, which is fifty-two euros and fifty cents.

Over long periods the difference becomes large. At the same rate, one thousand euros grows to about one thousand six hundred and thirty euros after ten years, and to about two thousand six hundred and fifty after twenty.

A quick way to estimate the effect is the rule of seventy-two: divide seventy-two by the yearly rate to get the number of years it takes for money to double. At six percent, money doubles in about twelve years.

The same rule works against you with debt. A credit card balance at twenty percent doubles in under four years if nothing is paid.
