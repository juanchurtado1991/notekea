What causes the tides

By the Example team

Tides are caused mainly by the gravity of the moon. The side of the earth facing the moon is pulled a little more than the centre, and the far side a little less, so the oceans bulge on both sides.

As the earth turns, most coasts pass through two bulges a day, which gives two high tides and two low tides about every twenty-four hours and fifty minutes.

The sun also pulls on the oceans, about half as strongly as the moon. When the sun and moon line up, at new and full moon, the tides are larger; these are spring tides. At half moon the tides are smaller; these are neap tides.

The shape of the coast changes the size of the tide a great deal. In the Bay of Fundy in Canada the difference between high and low water can reach sixteen metres.
