How solar panels make electricity

By the Example team

A solar panel is made of cells of silicon. When light hits a cell, it knocks electrons loose, and an electric field inside the cell pushes them in one direction, which makes a current.

The current is direct current. An inverter turns it into the alternating current used by homes and the grid.

Most panels sold today turn about twenty percent of the sunlight that falls on them into electricity. Heat lowers their output: a panel produces less on a hot summer afternoon than on a cold sunny morning.

Panels lose a little efficiency every year, usually less than one percent, and many manufacturers guarantee eighty percent of the original output after twenty-five years.
