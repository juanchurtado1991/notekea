Driving rovers on Mars

By the Example team

Rovers on Mars cannot be driven in real time. Depending on where the two planets are in their orbits, a radio signal takes between about four and twenty-four minutes to travel one way.

Instead, engineers send a list of commands once a day. The rover carries them out, takes pictures, and sends the results back so the team can plan the next day.

Newer rovers drive partly on their own. Perseverance builds a map of the ground from its cameras while it moves, and chooses a safe path around rocks without waiting for instructions.

Even so, rovers move slowly. Perseverance's top speed is about one hundred and fifty metres per hour, and on a typical day it drives a few hundred metres at most.
