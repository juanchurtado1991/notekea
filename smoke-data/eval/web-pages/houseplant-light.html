Light for houseplants

By the Example team

Light is the most common reason houseplants struggle. A spot that looks bright to people may be too dark for a plant, because the eye adjusts to dim rooms.

South-facing windows give the most light in the northern hemisphere, and suit cacti and succulents. East windows give gentle morning sun, which suits most leafy plants. North windows suit ferns and other plants from shaded forests.

Signs of too little light are long thin stems, small new leaves and leaning towards the window. Signs of too much are pale or scorched patches on the leaves.

Moving a plant just one metre away from a window can cut its light in half. Turning pots a quarter turn each week keeps growth even.
