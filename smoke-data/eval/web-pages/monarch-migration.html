The migration of monarch butterflies

By the Example team

Every autumn, millions of monarch butterflies fly from Canada and the United States to the mountains of central Mexico. Some travel more than four thousand kilometres.

No single butterfly makes the round trip. The generation that flies south lives about eight months, much longer than the summer generations, which live only a few weeks.

The butterflies find their way with the sun. An internal clock in their antennae corrects for the movement of the sun across the sky, so they keep flying south-west all day.

In Mexico they spend the winter clustered on oyamel fir trees, at about three thousand metres. In spring they mate and fly north, and their children and grandchildren continue the trip back to Canada.
