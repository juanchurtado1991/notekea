Why use a password manager

By the Example team

Most people reuse a few passwords across many sites. When one site is breached, attackers try the same email and password everywhere else; this is called credential stuffing.

A password manager creates a long random password for every site and fills it in for you. You only remember one strong master password.

Choose a manager that encrypts the vault on your device, so the company cannot read your passwords. Turn on two-factor authentication for the manager itself.

Start by changing the passwords of your email and bank accounts, since access to your email can be used to reset almost every other account.
