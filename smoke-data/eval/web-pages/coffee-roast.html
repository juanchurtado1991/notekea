Light and dark coffee roasts

By the Example team

Coffee beans are green before they are roasted. Roasting turns sugars and acids into hundreds of aroma compounds and makes the beans brown and brittle.

Light roasts are stopped soon after the first crack, a popping sound the beans make as water escapes. They keep more of the bean's own flavour, often fruity or floral, and taste more acidic.

Dark roasts go on to the second crack. They taste of the roast itself, with bitter and chocolate notes, and oils appear on the surface of the beans.

Contrary to a common belief, a dark roast does not have much more caffeine. Measured by weight, light and dark roasts contain about the same amount.
