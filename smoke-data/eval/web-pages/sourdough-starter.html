How to keep a sourdough starter alive

By the Example team

A sourdough starter is a mix of flour and water where wild yeasts and lactic bacteria live together. The yeasts make the gas that raises the bread, and the bacteria give it its sour taste.

To keep it alive, feed it regularly. A common routine is to discard most of the starter and add equal weights of flour and water, for example fifty grams of each, once a day at room temperature.

A starter kept in the fridge needs feeding only once a week. Take it out the day before baking and feed it twice, so it is active again. A healthy starter doubles in size within four to eight hours after a feeding.

If a grey liquid appears on top, the starter is hungry. Pour the liquid off or stir it in, and feed the starter. A pink or orange color, on the other hand, means it is spoiled and should be thrown away.
