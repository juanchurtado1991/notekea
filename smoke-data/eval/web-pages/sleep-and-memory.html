Why sleep helps memory

By the Example team

During deep sleep the brain replays what it learned during the day. Researchers recorded the activity of the hippocampus in rats and found that the same sequence of cells that fired while the animals ran a maze fired again, faster, while they slept.

This replay seems to move memories from the hippocampus, which stores them for a short time, to the cortex, where they last. People who sleep after studying remember more of a word list the next day than people who stay awake for the same hours.

Short nights have the opposite effect. After one night with five hours of sleep, students in one study remembered about a fifth fewer facts. Naps help too: a nap of sixty to ninety minutes, long enough to reach deep sleep, improved recall in several experiments.

The advice that follows is simple. Study before sleeping, keep regular hours, and avoid trading sleep for extra study time before an exam.
